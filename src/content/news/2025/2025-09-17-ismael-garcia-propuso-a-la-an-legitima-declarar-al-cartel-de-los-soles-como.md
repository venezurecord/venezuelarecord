Deputy Ismael García, a member of the Voluntad Popular (VP) caucus in the legitimate National Assembly, asked the Parliament in plenary session on Tuesday to formally declare the Cartel de los Soles a terrorist organization.

During his speech in the debate of the delegated committee, García argued that **the struggle in Venezuela is not only against a dictatorship, but against a criminal network linked to drug trafficking and international terrorism**.

**“The Cartel de los Soles is an absolutely perverse and harmful structure that operates from the highest levels of power, led by Nicolás Maduro** and sustained by military officers and civilians,” the VP lawmaker said.

He added that this network **controls not only drug trafficking, but also illegal activities related to gold**, which in his view makes the regime “an organized mafia with international reach.”

García recalled that in 2005 he began to denounce, with evidence presented in the Parliament itself, **the existence of the cartel, pointing to senior officials such as Diosdado Cabello, Néstor Reverol, Hugo “El Pollo” Carvajal and Henry Rangel Silva** as key pieces of the structure.

“We managed to prove before the Prosecutor’s Office of the Southern District of Florida and of New York **direct drug trafficking operations that even departed from the presidential ramp at Maiquetía,”** he said, alluding to a case of 5,600 kilos of cocaine transported on an Aeropostal plane.

**The VP deputy also recalled his complaints about links between the Cartel de los Soles and organizations such as the FARC and the ELN** in border areas, as well as the investigations that revealed money laundering networks in Panama, Spain and the United States tied to chavista leaders.

“We are not facing a conventional dictatorship, **but drug traffickers installed in power, responsible for having looted the country and for trafficking more than 450 tons of drugs a year from Venezuela,”** he said.

In his formal proposal, García urged the Parliament to join the recognition **that governments, parliaments and international bodies have already made of the existence of the Cartel de los Soles.**

**“It is time to speak out clearly and firmly to support the fight against drug trafficking and to reaffirm the investigations that were promoted from this Parliament,”** he stressed.

He concluded his speech with a message of confidence in the legislative work: **“I leave this proposal on the table. Venezuela will soon be free. Long live a free Venezuela.”**
